Why Broad Food Allergy Panels Can Be Misleading

The appeal of testing everything

When someone has unexplained stomach discomfort, rashes or headaches, it can be tempting to order a test covering dozens of foods. The promise sounds simple: find the culprit and remove it from your diet. Unfortunately, broad food allergy panels can produce results that are difficult to interpret and sometimes lead to restrictions that are not needed.

A positive result does not always mean allergy

Skin-prick and food-specific IgE blood tests identify sensitization to food proteins. Sensitization means the immune system recognizes a substance; it does not prove that eating it will cause a reaction. A person may test positive to a food they eat regularly without symptoms. Interpreting the result without their clinical history can cause unnecessary worry.

Why testing should follow your symptoms

An allergist usually begins by asking exactly what happened, which foods were involved and how quickly symptoms started. That history guides whether a particular test is worth doing. Testing foods without any suspected connection to symptoms raises the chance of confusing findings without providing a clearer diagnosis.

The problem with sensitivity panels

Commercial panels that measure food-specific IgG are sometimes marketed as a way to identify hidden food sensitivities. Allergy organizations generally do not recommend IgG testing to diagnose food allergy or intolerance. These antibodies can reflect ordinary food exposure, and using the results to eliminate large food groups may affect nutrition, particularly in children.

What if the diagnosis remains uncertain?

A clinician may suggest a targeted skin or blood test and, in selected cases, a medically supervised oral food challenge. An oral challenge is carried out where trained staff can respond to a reaction. It should never be attempted at home when an allergy is suspected.

A better starting point

Keep notes on meals, symptoms and their timing, then discuss them with a qualified professional. Ask what each proposed test would tell you and whether the result could meaningfully change your care.
