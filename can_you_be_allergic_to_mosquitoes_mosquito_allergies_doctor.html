Mosquito Bite Allergies: What's Normal and What's Not

Why mosquito bites cause bumps

Most people develop a small, itchy bump after a mosquito bite. The immune system reacts to proteins in mosquito saliva, producing inflammation around the bite. That common response is not the same as a dangerous allergic reaction.

Why some bites swell much more

Some people experience particularly large areas of redness, warmth, itching and swelling. This is sometimes called Skeeter syndrome. Children and people with limited prior exposure to certain mosquito species may react more strongly. Large local reactions can resemble a skin infection, so persistent or worsening symptoms should be assessed.

Ways to reduce discomfort

A cool compress may soothe the area. Try not to scratch, which can damage skin and increase the risk of infection. A pharmacist or clinician can advise on appropriate anti-itch treatments for your age and situation.

Preventing bites

Use window screens, wear suitable covering clothing and follow the label instructions for an age-appropriate insect repellent. Empty standing water around the home where mosquitoes may breed. These everyday steps also help reduce exposure to mosquito-borne illnesses.

When to ask for help

Seek medical advice if swelling continues to expand, the site becomes unusually painful, you develop fever or the skin looks infected. Breathing difficulty, fainting or widespread symptoms after a bite require emergency assistance, although such severe mosquito reactions are rare. Related reading: how allergic reactions work.
