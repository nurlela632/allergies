Five OTC Allergy Medicine Pitfalls to Avoid

A medicine is not simply good or bad

An allergy product may help one person but be a poor choice for another. Instead of labeling every over-the-counter medicine as the worst, it is more useful to look at situations where a particular product or habit creates problems. The following five pitfalls are worth discussing with a pharmacist.

1. Choosing a sedating tablet without thinking

Older antihistamines can cause pronounced sleepiness and reduced alertness. This matters for school, work, driving and other activities. They may also produce dry mouth or other side effects. Newer antihistamines are often less sedating, although they are not guaranteed to be completely non-drowsy.

2. Overusing decongestant nasal sprays

Certain nasal decongestants offer rapid relief but can cause rebound congestion if used longer than directed. The result may be a cycle of increasingly frequent spray use. Read the label and ask about alternatives if blockage persists.

3. Expecting tablets to fix every symptom

Oral antihistamines may help itching and sneezing but can be less effective for continuing nasal congestion. Nasal steroid sprays may better address inflammation when used correctly. Eye drops can be more suitable for symptoms mainly affecting the eyes.

4. Accidentally doubling ingredients

Combination remedies for colds and allergies sometimes include more than one active medicine. Taking them alongside another tablet or spray can lead to duplication and unwanted effects. Always compare ingredient lists rather than brand names alone.

5. Ignoring other health conditions

Some decongestants and other medicines are unsuitable for people with particular medical conditions or medication interactions. Age and pregnancy also matter. Ask a pharmacist before choosing a product if you are unsure, and do not change prescription treatments on your own.

Know when to seek more help

Persistent or worsening allergy symptoms deserve assessment. Serious breathing symptoms or fainting during a reaction require emergency care, not OTC treatment. Read allergy tablet differences and our rhinitis guide.

Making informed decisions

Keep track of your symptoms and discuss ongoing problems with a qualified professional. The choice of treatment should reflect your history and any other medical needs. Online reviews cannot replace an individual assessment.
