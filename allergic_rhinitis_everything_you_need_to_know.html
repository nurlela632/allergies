Allergic Rhinitis: Symptoms, Triggers and Relief

A constantly blocked nose can turn ordinary days into exhausting ones. Allergic rhinitis is inflammation inside the nose caused by an allergic reaction. It is often called hay fever when seasonal pollen is involved, but it can also occur throughout the year.

What does allergic rhinitis feel like?

Common symptoms include sneezing, a runny or stuffy nose, itching in the nose and watery eyes. Some people notice a drip at the back of the throat or find that poor sleep leaves them tired the next day. Symptoms can resemble a cold, but allergies typically do not cause a fever and may continue as long as you are exposed to the trigger.

Seasonal and year-round triggers

Tree, grass and weed pollens often cause symptoms during particular seasons. Dust mites, pet allergens and mold may lead to more persistent symptoms indoors. The timing and location of your symptoms can offer useful clues, although an assessment may be needed to identify what is responsible.

Reducing exposure without upending your routine

For pollen allergies, check local forecasts, keep bedroom windows shut when counts are high and change clothes after extended time outside. For dust mites, wash bedding regularly and consider allergen-resistant mattress and pillow covers. If pets trigger symptoms, keeping them out of sleeping areas may help. No single household change removes every allergen.

What treatments may help?

Saline nasal rinses, non-drowsy antihistamines and steroid nasal sprays are commonly used, depending on symptoms. Nasal steroid sprays are often particularly helpful for ongoing congestion, although they need consistent use. If you perform a nasal rinse, use distilled, sterile or previously boiled and cooled water. A pharmacist or clinician can guide you on appropriate products and technique.

When to see a specialist

Seek assessment if symptoms disturb your sleep, affect daily activities or do not improve with suitable treatment. Allergy testing and immunotherapy are options for some people. If you have asthma, discuss whether allergic triggers are also affecting your breathing.
