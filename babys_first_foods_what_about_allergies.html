Baby's First Foods: What Parents Should Know About Allergies

Starting solids brings a whole new set of questions. Along with choosing first foods and finding a routine that works for your family, you may be wondering how to introduce foods that commonly cause allergic reactions. The good news is that most babies can enjoy a wide variety of foods, including common allergens, once they are ready for solids.

When should babies try allergenic foods?

Most babies begin solid foods at around six months, when they can hold their head steady, sit with support and manage food in their mouth. There is generally no reason to delay introducing foods such as egg or peanut once a baby is developmentally ready. In fact, early introduction of peanut and egg during infancy can help lower the risk of developing allergies in some children.

If your baby has severe eczema, an existing food allergy or a previous reaction, speak with their clinician first. They may recommend a tailored plan or, in some circumstances, an allergy evaluation before introducing certain foods.

Which foods should you know about?

Common allergens include milk, egg, peanut, tree nuts, wheat, soy, fish, shellfish and sesame. These foods need to be prepared in a texture that is safe for a baby. Whole nuts are choking hazards; a little smooth peanut butter thinned with water or mixed into a familiar purée is a safer option. Egg should be thoroughly cooked, and fish should be cooked and carefully checked for bones.

What does a possible allergic reaction look like?

Reactions may include hives, swelling, vomiting, coughing or wheezing. They often begin soon after a food is eaten, but the timing can vary. Breathing difficulties, collapse or rapidly worsening symptoms may indicate a severe allergic reaction. Seek emergency help immediately. If your child has been prescribed epinephrine, follow their emergency action plan.

A calm approach to introducing new foods

Choose a time when your baby is well and you can watch for a reaction. Offer a small amount of one new allergenic food at a time, in a suitable texture, and see how they respond. Once a food is tolerated, keep it in your baby's diet regularly rather than trying it once and forgetting about it.

If a food causes symptoms that concern you, stop serving it and seek medical advice before trying it again. You do not need to introduce every new food in a single week: steady progress is perfectly fine.
