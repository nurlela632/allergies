Cat Allergy Facts: What Really Causes Your Symptoms?

The surprising cause of cat allergies

People often blame cat hair for their sneezing, but the fur itself is not usually the culprit. Cats produce allergenic proteins, including one called Fel d 1, in saliva and skin secretions. These proteins spread onto fur during grooming and then onto furniture, carpets, clothing and indoor air. That is why someone can notice symptoms even when the cat has left the room.

What symptoms might you notice?

Cat allergies can lead to itchy or watery eyes, sneezing, a blocked or runny nose and sometimes an itchy skin reaction after contact. In people with asthma, exposure can contribute to coughing or wheezing. Symptoms vary, and not everyone who sneezes near a pet necessarily has an allergy. Dust, fragrance and other household irritants can be involved too.

Are hypoallergenic breeds really different?

No cat breed has been proved to be completely hypoallergenic. Cats vary in how much allergen they produce, but a short coat or lack of fur does not guarantee someone will tolerate an animal. If you are considering adopting a cat and have a history of significant symptoms, speak with an allergy specialist before relying on a breeder's claims.

Steps that can reduce exposure

Keeping cats out of bedrooms may lower exposure where you sleep. Regularly washing bedding, wiping hard surfaces with a damp cloth and vacuuming with a HEPA-filtered machine can also help. A HEPA air cleaner may reduce particles in the air, although it does not eliminate allergens trapped in soft furnishings. Choose habits you can maintain rather than expecting one cleaning session to fix everything.

What treatment might help?

Depending on your symptoms, a pharmacist or healthcare professional may recommend antihistamines, suitable eye drops or a steroid nasal spray. An allergist can assess whether immunotherapy is appropriate for persistent symptoms. People with asthma should follow their asthma management plan and seek prompt help if breathing becomes difficult.

Making a realistic plan

Cat allergies do not have the same impact on everyone. Understanding your triggers and getting an accurate diagnosis can help you decide what changes are worthwhile. For more advice, read how to manage cat allergies and our allergic rhinitis guide.
