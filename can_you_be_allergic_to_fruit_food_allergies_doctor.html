Can You Be Allergic to Fruit? Symptoms and Causes

When fruit causes unexpected symptoms

Fruit is part of a balanced diet for many people, yet some notice an itchy mouth or throat after eating apples, peaches, kiwi or other varieties. A fruit allergy is possible, but not every uncomfortable sensation after fruit indicates an immune allergy.

The link with pollen allergies

One cause is pollen-food allergy syndrome, sometimes called oral allergy syndrome. Proteins in certain raw fruits resemble proteins in pollens, so the immune system can react to both. This often causes itching or tingling in the lips, mouth and throat shortly after eating. Some people can tolerate the same fruit cooked, but individual reactions vary.

Other types of fruit reactions

Fruit can also cause allergies unrelated to pollen, and these may involve hives, vomiting or other symptoms. Acidity and ordinary irritation can cause discomfort without an allergy. Because similar symptoms may have different causes, it is best not to guess from one experience.

When symptoms are serious

Breathing problems, faintness or rapidly worsening swelling after eating need emergency attention. If you have had a suspected allergic reaction, avoid the triggering fruit until a healthcare professional advises on safe next steps.

Finding a reliable answer

An allergist can review the food, preparation method, timing and your pollen allergy history. Testing may be useful in context, but results alone do not establish a clinical allergy. Learn more in our oral allergy syndrome article and food allergy basics.

This article offers general health information and does not replace individualized medical advice.
